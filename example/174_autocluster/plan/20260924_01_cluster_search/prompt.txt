# Methodische Vorgaben für das Clustering
Ich habe folgende frage mit einer anderen AI diskutiert
i heard we should cluster in higher dimension. how can i determine the best dimension parameter? what optimization criterion could i use?

Hier ist eine Antwort der AI:

To determine the optimal dimension parameter (`n_components`) for UMAP, you must avoid a common pitfall in unsupervised learning:

> **The Cardinal Rule:** Never evaluate clustering quality (e.g., Silhouette score, Davies-Bouldin) **inside the reduced UMAP space** when comparing across different dimensions.

If you test $d=2$ vs. $d=15$ and compute the silhouette score in their respective spaces, the scores are **incomparable**. In lower dimensions, points are artificially packed closer, and distance distributions change drastically. Furthermore, UMAP with `min_dist=0.0` aggressively collapses points into dense artificial pockets, easily inflating the low-dimensional silhouette score even when the underlying clusters make no semantic sense.

Here are the best criteria and methods to mathematically determine the optimal `n_components`.

---

### Strategy 1: The High-Dimensional Validation Criterion (Recommended)

The most reliable optimization criterion is to **cluster in the reduced $d$-dimensional space**, but **score the resulting cluster labels in the original 768-dimensional space**.

The 768-D embedding space is your ground-truth semantic space. If reducing to 10 dimensions allows DBSCAN to find clusters that are cohesive and well-separated in the original 768-D space, that dimension successfully captured the real manifold structure without creating false neighbor collisions.

#### The Optimization Objective:
$$\text{Score}(d) = \text{Silhouette}_{\text{768D}}(\mathbf{X}_{\text{orig}}, \mathbf{y}_d) \times (1 - \text{NoiseRatio})$$

* Normalize your 768-D vectors to unit length so Euclidean distance is mathematically equivalent to Cosine distance:
  $$\|u - v\|_2^2 = 2 - 2 \cos(u, v)$$
* Filter out DBSCAN noise (`-1`) before scoring.
* Penalize solutions that throw away too many video summaries as noise.

#### cuML Implementation:
```python
import cupy as cp
from cuml.manifold import UMAP
from cuml.cluster import DBSCAN
from cuml.metrics.cluster import silhouette_score

# 1. L2-Normalize original embeddings so Euclidean silhouette == Cosine silhouette
norms = cp.linalg.norm(X_gpu, axis=1, keepdims=True)
X_norm = X_gpu / cp.maximum(norms, 1e-12)

candidate_dims = [2, 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32]
dim_results = {}

for d in candidate_dims:
    # Reduce to candidate dimension d
    umap_model = UMAP(
        n_components=d, 
        n_neighbors=30, 
        min_dist=0.0, 
        metric='cosine', 
        random_state=42
    )
    X_reduced = umap_model.fit_transform(X_gpu)
    
    # Run DBSCAN (tune eps appropriately for dimension d)
    # Note: As dimension d increases, distance distributions widen, so eps must be adjusted
    dbscan = DBSCAN(eps=0.3 + (d * 0.02), min_samples=15)
    labels = dbscan.fit_predict(X_reduced)
    
    valid_mask = (labels != -1)
    noise_ratio = float(cp.mean(~valid_mask))
    n_clusters = int(len(cp.unique(labels[valid_mask])))
    
    if n_clusters > 1 and noise_ratio < 0.40:
        # CRUCIAL: Compute silhouette on X_norm (768D), NOT on X_reduced!
        score_768d = float(silhouette_score(X_norm[valid_mask], labels[valid_mask]))
        adjusted_score = score_768d * (1.0 - noise_ratio)
        
        dim_results[d] = {
            'score_768d': score_768d,
            'adjusted_score': adjusted_score,
            'clusters': n_clusters,
            'noise': noise_ratio
        }
        print(f"Dim {d:2d} -> 768D Silhouette: {score_768d:.4f}, Noise: {noise_ratio:.1%}, Clusters: {n_clusters}")
```

---

### Strategy 2: Manifold Fidelity Criteria (Trustworthiness & Continuity)

Before running DBSCAN, you can evaluate how well UMAP preserves topological relationships as dimension $d$ increases.

1. **Trustworthiness (`cuml.metrics.trustworthiness`):** Measures whether UMAP introduces **false neighbors** (points that were distant in 768D but ended up adjacent in $d$-D).
   * Range: $[0, 1]$. Values above 0.90–0.95 mean the low-dimensional projection is faithful.
2. **Neighborhood Preservation ($k$-NN overlap):** Fraction of a point's $k$-nearest neighbors in 768D that are still within its $k$-nearest neighbors in $d$-D.

#### How to use it: The Elbow Method
Plot **Trustworthiness vs. Dimension ($d$)**:

```
Trustworthiness
  1.0 |                   *---*---*  (Diminishing returns)
      |              *---*
 0.90 |         *---*
      |        /
 0.75 |       *  <-- 2D has high information loss / false neighbors
      +---------------------------------
        2   4   8   12  16  24  32   Dimension (d)
```
* At $d=2$, trustworthiness is often surprisingly low ($\approx 0.70 - 0.82$) for 768D text embeddings because you are squishing 768 orthogonal axes into 2.
* At $d \approx 8 - 15$, trustworthiness hits an inflection elbow ($\ge 0.93$), after which adding more dimensions yields diminishing returns while exponentially increasing clustering difficulty.

```python
from cuml.metrics import trustworthiness

for d in [2, 4, 8, 12, 16, 24]:
    X_reduced = UMAP(n_components=d, n_neighbors=30, min_dist=0.0).fit_transform(X_gpu)
    # Native GPU Trustworthiness
    t_score = trustworthiness(X_gpu, X_reduced, n_neighbors=30)
    print(f"Dimension {d:2d} | Trustworthiness: {t_score:.4f}")
```

---

### Strategy 3: Estimate the "Intrinsic Dimensionality" First

You do not need to guess search ranges blindly. Dense text embeddings (like Sentence Transformers or BERT) occupy a non-linear sub-manifold whose **intrinsic dimension (ID)** can be calculated analytically.

1. **The TwoNN Estimator:**
   Calculates intrinsic dimensionality based on the ratio of distances to the first and second nearest neighbors. For standard 768D text embeddings across open-domain YouTube summaries, the intrinsic dimension is typically between **8 and 22**.
2. **PCA / TruncatedSVD Variance Baseline:**
   Fit `cuml.decomposition.PCA` on `X_gpu` and calculate cumulative explained variance. Look for the number of components needed to retain 60%–80% of total variance. This establishes an empirical upper bound for UMAP.

---

### Why Not Just Use $d=50$ or $d=100$? (The Density Trap)

You might wonder: *If higher dimensions preserve more information, why stop at 10 or 15?*

This is governed by the **Curse of Dimensionality on Density Estimation**:
1. **Exponential Volume Growth ($V \propto r^d$):** As dimension $d$ grows, the space becomes exponentially empty. In 50D, all data points are approximately equidistant from each other, causing distance distributions to concentrate.
2. **DBSCAN Breakdown:** DBSCAN relies on finding a dense neighborhood within radius `eps`. Past $d \approx 20$, finding an `eps` that separates clusters without marking everything as noise becomes mathematically intractable.

### Summary Rule of Thumb for YouTube Embeddings

| Dimension ($d$) | Role & Tradeoff |
| :--- | :--- |
| **$d = 2$** | **Bad for clustering.** Severe information loss; forces semantically distinct video topics into overlapping clusters. Use only for final $(x, y)$ scatter plotting. |
| **$d = 5 \text{ to } 12$** | **The Sweet Spot.** Retains $>90\%$ of local manifold structure while keeping space dense enough for DBSCAN / HDBSCAN to find sharp boundaries. |
| **$d \ge 25$** | **Density breakdown.** Distance concentration sets in; DBSCAN will require very large `eps` and classify genuine clusters as noise. |

**Recommended Protocol:**
1. Check **Trustworthiness** across $d \in [2, 4, 8, 12, 16]$. Pick the lowest dimension that achieves $\ge 0.92$ trustworthiness.
2. Run your DBSCAN grid search at that dimension.
3. Validate candidate clusterings using **Silhouette score calculated on the original 768D normalized vectors**.
